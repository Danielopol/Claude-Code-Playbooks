Too many people use ChatGPT or Claude to write ad copy that will never convert for their offers.
That's not because the tools are bad, it's simply because you're giving them nothing to work with.
This is the exact system we use to get AI writing copy that sounds like it came from someone who's worked on your brand for years.
It takes about 30 minutes to set up once then it works forever.
The problem with how most people use AI for copy
You open ChatGPT, type "write me a UGC script for my product" and get back something that sounds like it was written by someone who's never seen your website.
Generic benefits, wrong tone, and no understanding of your customer.
That's because the AI has zero context on your brand, your offer, or who you're selling to.
The fix is building context that lives permanently in your AI workspace.
The system: ChatGPT Deep Research + Claude Projects
Here's what we do:
1. Use ChatGPT's Deep Research to build comprehensive docs on your offer and your customer
2. Export those as text files
3. Upload them into a Claude Project
4. Now every time you ask Claude to write something, it has full context on your brand
The difference in output quality is night and day.
Step 1: Build your offer characteristics doc
Open ChatGPT, make sure you're on the latest model, and select deep research.
You're going to prompt it to research everything about your product from a direct response angle:
- Product variants and pricing
- The big benefit
- Unique mechanism
- Features
- Functional benefits
- Emotional benefits
- Competitors and differentiators
The key is guiding it to the right product.
If you sell multiple things, pick one product or collection to focus on.
Give it:
- Offer name
- Offer type (single product or collection)
- Offer description
- The URL to your product page or collection page
- Any guidance to keep it focused (e.g., "adult sizes only, not kids")
Let it run (takes about 10-15 minutes)
When it's done, you'll have comprehensive direct response context on your offer that would take a human copywriter days to compile.
Step 2: Build your customer avatar doc
Same process, fresh ChatGPT thread, with deep research selected.
This time you're researching your customer:
- Who buys this product
- Their pain points
- Their desires
- Where they hang out online
- What language they use
- What objections they have
Paste in the offer context you just built so it knows what product we're talking about.
Tell it to index heavily into first-party sources like Reddit where real customers talk about products like yours.
Another 10-15 minutes and you'll have a complete avatar doc.
Step 3: Upload to Claude Projects
Take both docs, export them as .txt files from Google Docs.
Open Claude, create a new Project, and drag both files in.
Now add a system prompt. Something like:
"You are an ecommerce Facebook ads direct response copywriter. You must review the offer characteristics and customer avatar docs to thoroughly understand the product and target customer before writing. Your copy must speak specifically to the avatar's pain points and desires."
You can beef this out more over time, but this gets you 80% of the way there.
The difference in output
Without context, Claude writes generic copy that could be for any product.
With context, it writes copy that references specific product features, speaks to real customer pain points, and sounds like someone who actually understands your brand.
Same AI. Completely different output.
What you can do with this
Once the context is loaded, you're not limited to UGC scripts.
You can ask Claude to:
- Write a listicle landing page
- Generate 10 unique marketing angles for Facebook
- Write a 30-second UGC script using a specific angle
- Create us vs them comparison graphics
- Write email sequences
- Generate static ad copy
The context carries across everything.
The two-shot approach
For best results, don't just ask for a script straight away.
First prompt: "What are 10 unique marketing angles I could use to market the Oodie on Facebook?"
It'll generate angles like thermostat wars between couples, morning dread, work from home comfort, etc.
Second prompt: "Write a 30-second UGC video script using angle 5."
Now it's writing from a specific angle with full product and customer context.
The output is dramatically better than asking for a generic script.
