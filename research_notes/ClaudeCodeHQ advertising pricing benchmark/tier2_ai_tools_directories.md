# Tier 2 competitors: advertising, sponsorship and paid-listing prices at general AI tools directories (as of 2026-10-29)

**Method note for the report writer (read this first):** The research environment's network policy blocked every direct page fetch. Both `curl` and WebFetch were refused for theresanaiforthat.com, futurepedia.io, toolify.ai, topai.tools, futuretools.io, dang.ai, orangebot.ai and web.archive.org (the Wayback Machine). All findings below therefore come from **web-search result extracts**. Many of those extracts are of the official pricing pages themselves, but I could not open any page to check the live rendering. Confidence is graded on that basis:
- **Medium** = the official page's text appeared in search, with or without third-party agreement.
- **Low** = third-party sources only, or sources that conflict.
- No row is marked **high**, because no page could be opened directly.

All access dates are 2026-10-29, and all prices are in USD unless stated. To verify the figures, these domains (and web.archive.org) would need to be allowed in the session's network settings.

## Q1. Rate list by product (paid submission, featured listing, badge, homepage/category sponsor, search placement, newsletter, sponsored article, banner)

### Takeaway
Paid listings at the large directories are mostly **one-time fees**:
- TopAI.tools: $47 to $229
- Toolify: $99
- Futurepedia: $247 to $497
- There's An AI For That (TAAFT): $49 to $437

Recurring products are mostly priced on request, especially newsletter slots at the large directories (TAAFT's main newsletter slot is about $7,900 per issue on Passionfroot). The small self-serve directories (roughly 10k–30k visits/month), which are the closest comparables for ClaudeCodeHQ, publish Stripe-style rate cards:
- Homepage or featured slots: **$40–$500 per month**
- Sponsored listings: about **$49 per week**
- Newsletter mentions: about **$79 per issue**
- Fast-track listings: **$29–$99 one-time**

#### Third-party benchmark (not a directory's own rate)
- Adsly's calculator gives about **$155 per spot per month at 10K visits** for marketplaces and directories, and about **$225 per spot per month at 10K visits** for AI and machine learning sites. These are generic estimates. — [Adsly](https://adsly.io/calculator/marketplaces-directories)

### Inferences
- **One-time listings vs. recurring sponsorships**
  - The big directories sell mainly one-time or lifetime listings: TAAFT $49/$437, Futurepedia $247/$497, Toolify $99, TopAI $47/$229, Easy With AI $125, Tool Finder $29, Dang $49.
  - Their recurring and newsletter products are mostly "rates on request". The exceptions are TAAFT Highlight at $99/month and Dang Pro at $19/month.
  - The small directories mostly sell recurring slots: $40–$499 per 30 days, $49–$149 per week, or $79 per newsletter issue.
- **How price scales with traffic**
  - One-time listing prices barely scale with traffic. Directories with about 17K–2M visits charge roughly $29–$125, and only the top two (TAAFT, Futurepedia) reach $437–$497.
  - Short featured windows sit at **$229–$299 per 7 days** at directories with about 1.6M–2.3M visits (TopAI, Easy With AI).
  - Top homepage slots on small sites run **$299–$499 per 30 days** (aitoolsdirectory.com, about 19K) or **$199–$349 per month** (AI Tools HQ, about 20K self-reported).
  - The cheapest small sites charge **$40–$79 per month** (aitoolsboard, AI Agent Index, Chatbot Gallery).
- **Price per 1,000 visits:** small directories price much higher per 1K visits than large ones. They sell exclusivity and niche intent, not reach.
- **Implication for ClaudeCodeHQ** (about 26.8K visits / 49.1K page views per month):
  - The closest published comparables suggest **$49–$99 one-time** for an expedited or featured listing.
  - About **$49–$149 per week** for a sponsored listing or homepage slot.
  - About **$150–$500 per month** for an exclusive top homepage or site-wide slot.
  - About **$79+ per issue** for a newsletter mention, if a list exists.
  - This brackets Adsly's $155–$225 per spot per month at 10K visits.
  - The Claude Code niche focus could argue for the upper end, as AI Tools HQ does with "buyers".
- **Link labeling** is almost never disclosed in these sources; only Chatbot Gallery explicitly says dofollow. Reporting and analytics terms were not found for any directory.

### Gaps
- No live page could be opened, so prices that change often (TAAFT tiers, aitools.fyi, Easy With AI) could not be checked against the current rendering.
- Not publicly disclosed or not found:
  - Futurepedia, Future Tools and TopAI newsletter or banner rates.
  - Toolify's actual CPC.
  - Insidr sponsorship rates.
  - Category-sponsor prices at most sites.
  - Sponsored-article prices at every site.
- I found no source for an "AItoptools" or "There's a Tool for That" rate card.
- Link rel attributes and reporting deliverables are missing for nearly all products.

## Q3. Founding/launch discounts, sold-out signals, self-serve vs. apply

### Takeaway
- The big directories (Future Tools, TAAFT newsletter, Futurepedia sponsorships, Insidr) use **apply or quote** for recurring sponsorships.
- One-time listings are self-serve almost everywhere.
- Small directories are self-serve with Stripe-style checkout.
- Scarcity is used actively, through "sold out" labels, slot caps and "limited spots".

### Cited Findings
- **Sold-out signals**
  - Futurepedia's $247 Basic Listing is marked "Sold Out". — [Futurepedia submit-tool](https://www.futurepedia.io/submit-tool)
  - aitools.fyi sponsorships had "no spots available". — [Submitator](https://submitator.com/submit-to-aitools-fyi)

## Q5. Public disclosures of revenue or results (ROI of paid listings; directory-owner revenue)

### Takeaway
I found **no source with click or conversion data for a specific paid listing** at TAAFT or Futurepedia. The available ROI evidence is anecdotal and mostly negative on direct traffic. Directory-owner revenue disclosures for small AI directories range from about $1K all-time to about $8.7K in 30 days.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Buyers of AI-directory listings appear to pay mainly for backlinks, SEO and AI-assistant visibility rather than measured referral clicks.

- Small-directory revenue is lumpy: mostly one-time payments with little MRR. Recurring sponsor slots are what turn it into predictable income.
