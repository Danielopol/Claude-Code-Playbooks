## What This Does

Distills Apple's own WWDC design talks — chiefly *Designing Fluid Interfaces* — into a concrete, web-implementable set of rules for gesture-driven UI: kill input latency, track a drag 1:1 with the pointer's actual grab offset, and above all make every animation interruptible — grabbable and reversible mid-flight, always starting from the live on-screen value rather than snapping to a logical target. It covers the two-parameter spring model (damping ratio for overshoot, response for settle speed) that replaces the raw mass/stiffness/damping physics triplet, velocity handoff from a released gesture into its settle animation, and momentum projection — the exponential-decay math that makes a flick land where the gesture was actually heading, not just at the nearest boundary.

Beyond motion mechanics, it covers translucent materials and depth (when to blur, when to dim vs. offset, why never to stack two light translucent surfaces), multimodal feedback rules for combining motion with sound and haptics, concrete reduced-motion and reduced-transparency handling, and Apple's size-specific typography discipline (tracking and leading that scale inversely with font size). A closing section names the eight underlying design foundations — purpose, agency, responsibility, familiarity, flexibility, simplicity, craft, delight — that everything else in the skill exists to serve.

## Tips & Best Practices

- Interruptibility is worth getting right before anything else on this list — an interface that can be grabbed and reversed mid-flight reads as fluid even with fairly plain visuals; one that can't reads as stiff no matter how polished the rest is.
- Bounce is earned, not default — reserve it for motion the user's own gesture actually carried momentum into. A menu or modal that just appears should settle cleanly, with no overshoot.
- Design reduced-motion and reduced-transparency as real states, not an afterthought bolted on at the end — they change more than just "turn animations off."

## Limitations

- Web-platform techniques (CSS, Pointer Events, spring libraries) approximate native iOS motion closely but not perfectly — some effects have no exact web equivalent.
- Assumes access to a spring-capable animation library; the raw math (projection, rubber-banding) can be hand-implemented, but a good spring implementation is worth reaching for rather than rebuilding from scratch.
- These are interface-motion principles, not a full design system — pair them with your project's actual visual language, brand, and component library.
