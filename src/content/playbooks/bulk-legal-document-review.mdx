## What This Does

Converts a pile of legal documents into a single structured spreadsheet you can actually sort, filter, and compare — instead of forty open PDFs and a notepad. Each document becomes a row, each review question becomes a column, and every cell carries both the extracted answer and a citation back to the exact clause it came from.

## What You Get

A multi-sheet Excel workbook:

| Sheet | Contents |
|-------|----------|
| Summary | Review name, document count, risk distribution, color legend |
| Review Matrix | The main grid — documents as rows, extraction questions as columns, risk cells color-coded Green/Yellow/Red |
| Sources | Clause/page reference for every extracted value |
| Extraction Parameters | The list of columns and the exact question each one answers |

## Pre-Built Column Sets

The template ships ready-to-use extraction columns for five common document types — commercial/master service agreements, NDAs, leases, loan agreements, and share/asset purchase agreements — each with 10–16 fields tuned to what actually matters for that document type. All are fully customizable.

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Never leave a cell blank.** "Not addressed" or "Silent" is a finding — the absence of a liquidated damages clause is information, not a gap in the work.
- **Attach a source to every value.** A risk flag without a clause reference isn't verifiable and won't survive a lawyer's spot-check.
- **Normalize terminology across rows.** If one contract says "12 months" and another says "1 year" for the same concept, write them the same way — otherwise the matrix can't actually be compared or sorted.
- **Batch large sets.** For 10+ documents, process in batches and verify consistency after each one. For 50+, prioritize a subset (e.g. highest contract value first) rather than processing everything in one uneven pass.
- **Spot-check before delivering.** Re-read 2–3 documents against their extracted rows to catch systematic extraction errors before they propagate across the whole matrix.

## Limitations

- Extraction quality depends on document quality — scanned PDFs without clean OCR will produce weaker results than searchable text
- Risk classifications (Green/Yellow/Red) are only as good as the negotiation playbook or market standard they're measured against — without one, the workbook should flag that it's using general market norms rather than your organization's actual position
- This produces a working analytical document for legal review, not a substitute for it — every extraction and risk flag needs a qualified lawyer's sign-off before anyone relies on it
