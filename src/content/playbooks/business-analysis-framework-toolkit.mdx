## What This Does

Splits consulting-grade report writing into two disciplined phases. **Phase 1** takes a research subject and domain (market, brand, financial, industry, consumer, investment, or competitive), selects 2–4 complementary frameworks from a catalog spanning strategic analysis (SWOT, PESTEL, Porter's Five Forces), market and growth (BCG Matrix, Ansoff, TAM-SAM-SOM), consumer behavior (RFM, Consumer Decision Journey, Jobs-to-be-Done), financial valuation (DCF, DuPont, Comparable Company Analysis), and competitive positioning (Blue Ocean, Strategic Group Mapping) — then builds a chapter skeleton where every chapter names its objective, its framework, its hypothesis, its exact data requirements, and its visualization plan.

Only after real data is collected against that plan does **Phase 2** synthesize the final report, under one non-negotiable rule: the **Data Authenticity Protocol**. Every number and chart must trace to actually-collected data — missing data gets flagged as "not available," never invented. Each section follows a fixed narrative chain — Observation (What) → Attribution (Why) → Implication (So What) — so the report reads as interpreted insight in a consulting voice, not a data dump.

## Tips & Best Practices

- Resist starting Phase 2 with a thin data package "to keep momentum" — a report built on gaps just relocates the gap-filling problem into hallucinated-sounding prose. Close the required data gaps first.
- The framework-selection step is where reports most often go wrong: two frameworks that measure the same thing (Porter's Five Forces and Strategic Group Mapping can easily collapse into one section) waste a chapter instead of adding depth — be deliberate about what each one uniquely contributes.
- Keep the consolidated data-collection task list deduplicated across chapters — two chapters often want the same market-size figure; request it once.

## Limitations

- This builds the framework and the final report — it is explicitly not a data-collection or web-research tool, and needs a real research step (an agent or a human) between its two phases.
- The framework catalog favors business and strategy domains; a genuinely technical or scientific research report needs different tooling.
- Best suited to reports with real data behind them — for a quick, low-stakes internal memo, this level of structure is more overhead than the task warrants.
