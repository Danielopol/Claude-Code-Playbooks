## What This Does

A structured plan review that refuses to rubber-stamp: pick one of four review postures (Scope Expansion, Selective Expansion, Hold Scope, or Scope Reduction), then apply nine prime directives — zero silent failures, every error named, all shadow paths traced, edge cases mapped, observability as scope not afterthought, diagrams mandatory, deferred items written down, six-month-future thinking, permission to say "scrap it" — plus a set of CEO-level cognitive patterns (inversion reflex, focus as subtraction, proxy skepticism, edge-case paranoia) to catch what a quick skim misses.

The mode is chosen once and held for the whole review — no silent drift toward a different posture halfway through.

## The Four Modes

| Mode | Posture |
|------|---------|
| Scope Expansion | Envision the platonic ideal — push scope up, propose 10x improvements, but every expansion is your decision |
| Selective Expansion | Hold the current scope as baseline and make it bulletproof, while surfacing expansion opportunities one at a time to cherry-pick |
| Hold Scope | Maximum rigor on the scope as given — no silent expansion or reduction |
| Scope Reduction | Find the minimum viable version and cut everything else, ruthlessly |

## The Nine Prime Directives

Zero silent failures · Every error has a name · Shadow paths traced (nil, empty, upstream error, plus happy path) · Edge cases mapped for every interaction · Observability as first-class scope · Diagrams mandatory for non-trivial flows · Deferred items written down · Six-month-future thinking · Permission to say "scrap it and do this instead"

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Choose the mode before the review starts, and hold it.** Silent drift between modes — starting in Expansion and quietly reviewing like Hold Scope by section three — is the most common way this review loses its value. Raise concerns about the mode itself once, up front, then execute it faithfully.
- **"Handle errors" is not a plan item — name the specific exception.** A catch-all handler is a code smell worth flagging explicitly, not a detail to let slide because the plan mentions error handling in general terms.
- **Trace all four paths for every new data flow**, not just the happy path: nil input, empty/zero-length input, and upstream error are the three shadow paths that most plans skip.
- **Observability and rollback planning are scope, not cleanup.** If they're not explicit line items in the plan, that's a finding worth raising, not something to assume will happen later.
- **Deliver a plain verdict, not just a list of observations.** The review ends with an actual recommendation — ship as-is, ship with specific changes, or reconsider — not a pile of findings with no synthesis.

## Limitations

- This is a review discipline for genuinely consequential plans — applying full rigor to a one-line config change wastes the reviewer's time and yours
- The cognitive-patterns framework is a way of thinking through the review, not a checklist to enumerate in the output — over-literal application (listing all eleven patterns explicitly) misses the point of internalizing them
- Best suited for plans that already exist in some written form; this reviews a plan, it doesn't generate one from scratch — pair it with a design/brainstorming playbook for that earlier step
