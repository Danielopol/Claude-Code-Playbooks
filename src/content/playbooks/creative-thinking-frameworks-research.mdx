## What This Does

Eight distinct, cognitive-science-grounded frameworks for research ideation, each targeting a different mental operation and each backed by real creativity research rather than ad-hoc brainstorming prompts: Koestler's bisociation for combining distant domains via a systematic cross-product matrix, Gestalt-style problem reformulation for re-representing a problem instead of just attacking it as stated, Gentner and Dunbar's structure-mapping theory for analogies that transfer real causal mechanisms (not surface metaphors), Boden's constraint-transformation framework for finding the hidden assumptions most fertile for a genuine paradigm shift, TRIZ-style systematic negation of core field assumptions, abstraction laddering (generalize, specialize, analogize), Kauffman's adjacent possible for timing an idea against what's *just* become feasible, and Rothenberg's Janusian thinking for synthesizing a real contradiction instead of picking a side.

A four-phase combined protocol walks a full ideation session from mapping the space through generating disruptions to deepening promising leads and evaluating survivors against a two-sentence test. A block-diagnosis table routes six common failure modes — fixation, tunnel vision, self-censoring, incrementalism, analysis paralysis, false dichotomy — to the specific framework built to unstick each one, so the session applies the right tool instead of running through all eight regardless of what's actually happening.

## Tips & Best Practices

- Generative mode first, evaluative mode second — filtering too early is the single most common way a creative session collapses back into incrementalism.
- Distant analogies are more valuable than nearby ones, but need more validation work before trusting them — don't skip the structural-fidelity check just because a distant analogy feels exciting.
- Bring real domain expertise to every framework here — none of these substitute for knowing the field; they scaffold thinking about it differently, they don't replace it.

## Limitations

- Ideation frameworks, not evaluation or prioritization tools — pair with a structured brainstorming or research-planning process for converging on what to actually pursue.
- Best suited to genuinely open-ended ideation; a well-scoped problem that just needs execution doesn't benefit from this level of cognitive scaffolding.
- Generates candidates without validating feasibility, novelty against the existing literature, or resource requirements — treat survivors as hypotheses worth investigating, not finished research directions.
