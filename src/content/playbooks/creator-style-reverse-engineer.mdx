## What This Does

Based on a workflow from The Startup Ideas Podcast: feed an AI agent a short video with one prompt, and get a full creative playbook back.

> Break down the style, transcribe the script, and give me a replication plan.

In the original example, a 65-second video came back as a six-step plan covering the concept hook, talking-head setup, three types of B-roll, VFX explainers, a typography system, and a five-act edit. The post says the agent captured the typography, lighting, music tempo, and emotional arc. Then the real exercise: **do this for 10 creators you admire, mix what you like from each, and that's how you build your own style.**

This playbook turns that into a repeatable workflow in Claude Code:

1. **Break down the style.** Concept, on-camera look, B-roll, graphics, typography, pacing, and emotional arc, with timestamps.
2. **Transcribe the script** and summarize its structure.
3. **Write a replication plan** in the same six-step shape, adapted to your subject.
4. **Save it** to a `styles/` folder.
5. **Blend** several creators' breakdowns into your own style guide.

## Getting Claude Something It Can Analyze

Claude has no direct video or audio input, so the workflow depends on getting it the right evidence:

- **Transcript.** Supply one, or let Claude use a tool that makes one from the video.
- **Frames.** Claude looks at still images pulled from the video at scene changes and regular intervals. The site's **Claude Video** playbook sets up a script that does exactly this (frames plus a timestamped transcript). If you have `ffmpeg` installed, Claude can also extract frames itself. Claude asks before installing anything.
- **Music and sound.** Claude can't hear. It estimates tempo only from a tool's output or from your description, and otherwise marks music as **not assessed**.

The template makes Claude label each observation as **observed** (with a timestamp), **inferred**, or **not assessed**, so you know what's solid. This matters for claims like "music tempo," which the original post attributes to the agent but which depend on how the video's audio actually reaches it.

## The Replication Plan

The six-step skeleton from the original example, adapted to each video:

| Step | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Concept hook | One sentence, explainable in under 10 seconds, with a visual payoff you can show immediately |
| 2. Talking head | Setting, lighting, orientation, and what to hold or show on camera |
| 3. B-roll | The specific shot types to capture and how to light them |
| 4. Explainer graphics | The look, the tools, and a speed-versus-control tradeoff |
| 5. Typography system | Title card, section headers, and captions, defined before editing |
| 6. Edit structure | Hook → Conflict → Build → Resolution → CTA, with timings |

The details in the original (dark room, RGB lighting, vertical shooting, line art with glow on black) were specific to that one video. Claude uses the steps as a frame and fills them with what your chosen video actually does. Each step comes with the reason it works, so you learn the technique.

## Building Your Own Style

After a few videos, ask Claude to compare your `styles/` folder. It builds a table of techniques across creators, asks which ones you like from each (the choice is yours), and writes a `my-style-guide.md` that resolves conflicts, such as two incompatible lighting looks, and explains each decision. It then suggests a small first test video.

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Start with one tight video.** Under a couple of minutes gives clean, specific results. A long video blurs the breakdown.
- **Pick creators for different strengths.** One for hooks, one for graphics, one for pacing. Mixing works best when each contributes something specific.
- **Give Claude your subject.** The replication plan is far more useful when it's written for your actual topic, gear, and budget.
- **Check the frames yourself.** For anything you plan to copy, such as a lighting setup or a transition, look at the frame Claude cites.
- **Test small.** Shoot a short test following the plan before committing to a full video.
- **Use timestamps.** Ask Claude to cite them, so you can verify any claim.

## Limitations

- **Claude can't watch or hear the video directly.** It works from frames, a transcript, and tool output. Music, sound design, and voice delivery are marked not assessed unless you supply evidence.
- **Equipment and software are guesses.** Claude can say a look resembles a certain lighting setup or effects style, but it can't know what camera, light, or app was actually used.
- **A breakdown isn't a recipe for success.** It describes what the video did, not why it performed. Creators succeed for reasons a single video can't show.
- **Copy techniques, not content.** The template tells Claude not to reproduce a creator's script, footage, music, or branding, and to suggest ways to make signature elements your own. Respect each platform's terms when downloading videos, and make sure you have the right to analyze the content.
- **The original example is one video.** The six steps, and details like dark rooms and glow effects, came from one hardware-focused creator. Your breakdowns will differ.
