## What This Does

Turns Claude Code into an Export Administration Regulations (EAR, 15 CFR 730-774) compliance advisor. It handles ECCN classification across all 10 CCL categories and 5 product groups, EAR99 determination, jurisdiction analysis (EAR vs. ITAR order of review), Country Chart license analysis, all license exceptions (LVS, GBS, TMP, RPL, TSU, ENC, and more), end-user/end-use controls (Entity List, Denied Persons, Unverified, MEU), deemed exports, the Foreign Direct Product Rule (FDPR), de minimis thresholds, the 10 General Prohibitions, SNAP-R applications, voluntary self-disclosure, and Export Compliance Program (ECP) design.

---

## The Problem

The EAR reaches far more companies than they expect: dual-use hardware, software, and technology can require licenses depending on the item, destination, end-user, and end-use. ECCN classification is technical, the Country Chart logic is non-obvious, and end-user screening, deemed exports, and the FDPR add layers most teams aren't equipped to handle — until BIS comes asking.

## Quick Start

### Step 1: Create Your Workspace

```bash
mkdir -p ~/Documents/EAR-Compliance
```

### Step 2: Download the Template

```bash
mv ~/Downloads/CLAUDE.md ~/Documents/EAR-Compliance/
```

### Step 3: Add Context (Optional)

Add product specifications, technology descriptions, destination countries, and customer/end-user details.

### Step 4: Run Claude Code

```bash
cd ~/Documents/EAR-Compliance
claude
```

### Step 5: Start

Say: **"Classify our product's ECCN and run a license-requirement analysis."**

## Tips

- **Classify before you ship** — ECCN drives the entire license analysis.
- **Screen every end-user** — restricted-party lists change frequently.
- **Watch deemed exports and the FDPR** — they catch "domestic-only" companies.

---

## Important Disclaimer

This is a compliance support tool, not legal advice. EAR violations carry significant penalties. Have qualified export-control counsel review ECCN and licensing determinations before acting.
