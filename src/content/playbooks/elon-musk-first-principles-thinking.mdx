## What This Does

A thinking-partner mode built from documented, public engineering philosophy across SpaceX and Tesla: physics-based reasoning ("physics is the law, everything else is a recommendation"), the mandatory five-step engineering process (question the requirement, delete the part, simplify what's left, accelerate the cycle, automate last — skipping ahead to automation before deletion is treated as the classic, well-documented failure mode), the "idiot index" as a fast cost-sanity-check, and the 10%/10x/100x distinction for judging whether an idea is worth pursuing on its own math rather than on how inspiring the pitch sounds. It also covers documented hiring, meeting, and execution-culture practices — evaluating for demonstrated ability over credentials, cutting meetings that don't need to exist, and a real preference for a short written memo over a slide deck.

This mode is **deliberately scoped** to engineering, manufacturing, and execution philosophy — domains with a large, stable, well-documented public record — and explicitly declines to simulate current political positions, government commentary, or personal/psychological territory, redirecting instead to the underlying technical or organizational question if there is one. A standing reminder ships with the mode: this is a simulation built from public material, never the real person's actual current views, repeated whenever a session resumes rather than stated once and forgotten.

## Tips & Best Practices

- The five-step engineering process is the single most reusable piece of this mode — it applies cleanly to software architecture decisions, org-design questions, and process bloat well outside literal manufacturing.
- The idiot index is a fast, genuinely useful gut-check for any cost or pricing question — compute it explicitly (final cost ÷ raw input cost) rather than just eyeballing whether something "feels expensive."
- Resist the pull to make this a general-purpose opinion generator on anything the real person has ever publicly commented on — the deliberately bounded scope (engineering and execution, not politics or personal life) is what keeps this a useful thinking tool rather than a misattribution risk.

## Limitations

- A simulation built from public engineering interviews, documented company practices, and reporting — not the real person, not authorized by him, and not a source for his actual current views on anything, especially outside the deliberately bounded engineering/execution scope.
- Best suited to hard technical, manufacturing, and organizational-execution questions — this is intentionally narrower than the full range of topics the real person has publicly commented on.
- The standing disclaimer and the political/personal-topic decline are deliberate design choices — don't let a long session quietly drop either just because they already came up once.
