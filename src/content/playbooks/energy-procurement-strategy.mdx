## What This Does

Distills the working knowledge of a senior commercial and industrial energy procurement manager into decision frameworks for the core recurring decisions: how much price risk to retain versus transfer to suppliers (fixed vs. index vs. layered), how to identify and mitigate demand charges (the most controllable line item on most bills), how to evaluate a PPA offer for basis risk and curtailment exposure, and how to report procurement decisions to three different audiences — finance (budget variance), sustainability (Scope 2 progress), and operations (real constraints) — without restating the same numbers three flat ways.

It covers utility bill anatomy (why energy, demand, capacity, and T&D charges need independent analysis rather than one blended "rate"), the specific mechanics of demand-charge ratchet clauses and capacity-tag calculations that catch procurement teams off guard, and a set of key edge cases drawn from real market events — Winter Storm Uri's index-pricing exposure, VPPA basis risk in congested zones, and the demand-charge ratchet trap — each with the lesson it actually teaches.

## Tips & Best Practices

- Never try to "call the bottom" of the energy market. Watch the forward curve against its 5-year historical range and accelerate or decelerate your layering schedule accordingly, rather than trying to time a single optimal purchase.
- Always check a tariff for ratchet provisions before any facility modification that could spike peak load — a single accidental peak can lock in elevated billing demand for up to 11 months.
- Quantify the same procurement decision three different ways for three different stakeholders: budget impact and variance for finance, Scope 2 progress for sustainability, operational constraints for operations.

## Limitations

- Decision-support knowledge, not a substitute for a licensed energy broker, legal counsel on contract terms, or a qualified risk-management function for large notional hedges.
- Market structures and ISO/RTO rules referenced are US-centric; other jurisdictions have materially different regulatory architectures.
- Dollar figures and rate ranges are illustrative benchmarks — always verify against current forward curves and your utility's actual tariff sheet before acting on them.
