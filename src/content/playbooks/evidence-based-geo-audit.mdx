## What This Does

Runs a structured audit of how citable a page is to AI search systems — Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, ChatGPT web search, and Perplexity — scored across five weighted criteria: citability, structural readability, multi-modal content, authority signals, and technical accessibility.

What sets this apart from typical GEO advice is the evidence discipline: every non-obvious claim traces to a named primary source (Google's own documentation, specific Google engineers, or large-sample third-party studies), and it explicitly corrects the most common GEO myth — that `llms.txt` is a ranking lever. It isn't, and Google's docs say so directly.

## The Five Scoring Criteria

| Criterion | Weight | What it checks |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|
| Citability | 25% | Self-contained 134-167 word answer blocks, direct answers up front, specific sourced data |
| Structural readability | 20% | Heading hierarchy, question-based headers, short paragraphs, tables and lists |
| Multi-modal content | 15% | Images, video, infographics, interactive elements |
| Authority & brand signals | 20% | Author credentials, publish/update dates, recency, entity presence (Wikipedia, Reddit, YouTube) |
| Technical accessibility | 20% | Server-side rendering, AI crawler access, llms.txt, RSL licensing |

## The llms.txt Myth, Corrected

| Source | What they actually said |
|--------|---------------------------|
| Google's AI optimization guide | Google Search ignores llms.txt entirely — it "won't harm (nor help)" rankings |
| John Mueller (Google) | Called it "a dead end"; compared it to deprecated meta keywords |
| SE Ranking, 300k-domain study | Only 1 of the top 50 AI-cited domains had an llms.txt |
| OtterlyAI server-log audit | 0.1% of AI-bot traffic requests it |

It does matter for AI *coding agents* reading developer documentation — just not for AI search citation. The audit reports presence without pretending it's a ranking factor.

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Recency is one of the highest-leverage levers available.** Content under 3 months old is roughly 3x more likely to be cited than older content, and pages stale for 6+ months start losing citation eligibility — a scheduled refresh program often beats a full rewrite.
- **Score Google AI Overviews and AI Mode separately.** They're different citation engines that agree on conclusions ~86% of the time but cite the *same URL* only ~13.7% of the time — optimizing for one doesn't automatically cover the other.
- **Server-side rendering is a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have.** AI crawlers don't execute JavaScript — client-only rendered content is frequently invisible to them regardless of how good the content itself is.
- **Brand mentions beat backlinks for AI visibility.** YouTube and Reddit mentions correlate far more strongly with AI citation than Domain Rating does (Ahrefs study) — if link building isn't moving the needle on AI visibility, that's expected, not a sign something's broken.
- **User-triggered AI fetchers ignore robots.txt by design.** Google-Agent, Google-NotebookLM, and ChatGPT-User act on a specific user's behalf and cannot be blocked via robots.txt — if access control matters here, it needs to happen server-side.

## Limitations

- Several cited statistics are third-party-reported (Ahrefs, SE Ranking, OtterlyAI) rather than confirmed by the platform owners — the audit flags which is which, and that distinction should be preserved when sharing findings
- GEO measurement is inherently noisier than traditional rank tracking — treat scores as directional, not a precise metric to optimize to the decimal point
- Optional integration with DataForSEO's AI-optimization tools exists for real-time ChatGPT/LLM mention tracking, but the core audit works fully without any paid API
