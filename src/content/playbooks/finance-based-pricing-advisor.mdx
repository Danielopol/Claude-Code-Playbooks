## What This Does

Walks through 4 adaptive questions — change type, expected impact, current baseline, recommendation — and delivers one of 4 patterns: Implement Broadly, Test First (A/B), Modify Approach, or Don't Change. Every recommendation includes math: ARPU lift, churn-driven revenue loss, net MRR impact, CAC payback change.

Not value-based pricing design. A financial impact lens for pricing changes you're already considering.

## Pricing Change Types

- **Direct monetization** — Price increase, new premium tier, paid add-on, usage-based pricing
- **Discount strategies** — Annual prepay, volume, promotional
- **Packaging changes** — Bundling, unbundling, pricing metric shift (seats → usage)

---

## Four Recommendation Patterns

| Pattern | Trigger | Output |
|---------|---------|--------|
| **Implement Broadly** | >10% ARPU lift, <5% churn risk | Math + grandfathering plan + monitoring thresholds |
| **Test First (A/B)** | Uncertain impact, moderate risk, large base | Cohort design + duration + decision criteria |
| **Modify Approach** | Original has risk, better alternative | Smaller increase / grandfather-only / segment-based pricing |
| **Don't Change** | Net negative or marginal, high churn risk | Reject + what would change + retention-first alternative |

---

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Grandfather existing customers.** Raise prices for new only — protects base from retaliatory churn.
- **Model conservative + base + optimistic.** Churn impact is always uncertain — don't plan around a point estimate.
- **Calculate net MRR impact, not gross.** +$75K MRR from price lift minus -$9.75K from accelerated churn is the number that matters.
- **Factor CAC payback.** Higher ARPU + lower conversion can make payback worse, not better.
- **Communicate 30-60 days in advance.** Surprise pricing changes drive churn more than the pricing itself.

---

## Common Pitfalls

- Ignoring churn impact (raise 30%, model zero churn response)
- Applying changes to all customers without grandfathering
- A/B testing with 10 customers (no statistical power)
- Raising prices without corresponding value communication
- Copycat pricing based on what a competitor did
- Heavy annual discounts (>15%) that destroy LTV for cash flow
- Forgetting the expansion revenue alternative (land and expand)
