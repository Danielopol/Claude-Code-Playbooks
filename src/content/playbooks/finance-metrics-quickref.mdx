## What This Does

Fast lookup for SaaS finance metrics: formula, good benchmark, red flag, action to take. Organized by category (revenue & growth, unit economics, capital efficiency, efficiency ratios) plus four decision frameworks (feature investment, channel scaling, pricing changes, business health by stage).

Not a teaching tool. A scannable reference for speed — use deep-dive skills (`saas-revenue-growth-metrics`, `saas-economics-efficiency-metrics`) to learn.

## Four Decision Frameworks

| Framework | Key Metrics | Core Question |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Build a Feature?** | Revenue, Gross Margin, LTV, Contribution Margin | Is ROI >3x or LTV impact >10x? |
| **Scale a Channel?** | CAC, LTV:CAC, Payback, Magic Number, NRR by channel | Is LTV:CAC >3:1 AND Payback <18mo? |
| **Change Pricing?** | ARPU, Churn, NRR, CAC Payback | Net positive after churn risk? |
| **Business Health?** | Growth %, NRR, LTV:CAC, Rule of 40, Magic Number | Stage-appropriate metrics hit? |

---

## Business Health by Stage

**Early (Pre-$10M ARR):** Growth >50% YoY, LTV:CAC >3:1, Gross Margin >70%, Runway >12 mo
**Growth ($10M-$50M ARR):** Growth >40%, NRR >100%, Rule of 40 >40, Magic Number >0.75
**Scale ($50M+ ARR):** Growth >25%, NRR >110%, Rule of 40 >40, Profit Margin >10%

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Use cohort-level, not blended.** Company averages hide degrading cohorts.
- **Always pair Quick Ratio with Churn.** High new MRR can mask a leaky bucket.
- **Check payback in months, not LTV:CAC alone.** LTV:CAC 5:1 with 36-month payback is still a cash trap.
- **Benchmarks vary by stage.** "Good" for a $2M ARR company is different from "good" for $50M.
- **Pick a decision question first.** Tracking 25 metrics without a question = analysis paralysis.

---

## Common Pitfalls

- Using blended averages instead of cohort/channel/segment
- Scaling acquisition when retention is quietly deteriorating
- Treating high LTV:CAC as sufficient (ignoring payback and runway)
- Comparing benchmarks across mismatched stages
- Raising prices on ARPU math alone (no churn/contraction modeling)
- Tracking metrics without a specific decision they inform
