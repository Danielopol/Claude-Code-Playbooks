## What This Does

Enriches **people you already have**: one named professional, or everyone in a list or CSV. It fills requested fields and never adds new prospects. To discover new people, use the Firecrawl lead-finding playbook.

How it works:

1. **Look up the professional** through Firecrawl's people data providers, starting from your identifiers and requested fields. Claude chooses the cheapest read-only path that answers the question, reading each provider's input, output, and billing contract first.
2. **Resolve identity before enriching.** It uses the strongest identifier a provider accepts (provider person ID, profile URL, or email). For a name, it includes the employer and location context, and resolves a company domain rather than guessing one. It checks match-confidence metadata against your context, because a successful response alone doesn't establish a match. For a single lead whose identity stays uncertain, it shows up to three candidates and asks you to choose. It never enriches a list of guesses or merges conflicting profiles.
3. **Execute the selected enrichment** and inspect each result. Personal-email and phone-reveal options stay disabled. A work email is shown only if requested and returned as an actual address (masked values, unlock placeholders, and availability flags aren't addresses).
4. **Return a contact card** (one lead) or an enriched table or CSV (many), with requested fields, relevant employment history, field-level source or provider attribution, profile links, and when checked. Supplied values are preserved unless you asked for corrections, conflicts are shown separately, and stale roles are flagged.

## Handling Lists and CSVs

- **Column mapping.** Claude maps your columns to supported identifiers and requested fields, asking only if an ambiguous mapping blocks the work.
- **Nothing gets dropped.** Row IDs, order, duplicates, and every unmatched or failed row are preserved. Duplicates aren't collapsed, and row-specific supplied values aren't overwritten.
- **Ambiguous rows stay unresolved.** Claude continues with independent matches without pausing to ask about each ambiguous row, and doesn't replay successful lookups to recover failures.
- **Status per row.** Match, no match, ambiguity, or error.
- **Safe batching.** Batches are used only as the provider's contract supports and within your limits. Responses are mapped by identifier or documented correspondence, never assumed response order.
- **CSV done properly.** Output uses a CSV writer so quoting and embedded newlines survive.

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Include the strongest identifier you have.** A profile URL or email resolves identity better than a bare name.
- **State your limits.** Row counts, credit budgets, and fields needed. Claude stays within them.
- **Ask for freshness.** Record-update dates are shown when the provider supplies them, and are kept distinct from lookup time. Unavailable freshness is "unknown."
- **Review conflicts.** Where two sources disagree, both appear with their sources.

---

## Limitations

- **Professional context only.** Home addresses and sensitive personal information are excluded.
- **No CRM writes or outreach.** Claude doesn't create CRM records, enroll contacts in sequences, or send messages.
- **No claims of verification.** Provider verification labels are preserved, but Claude doesn't claim independent verification or guaranteed deliverability.
- **Provider access varies.** If a provider is unavailable or incomplete, Claude falls back to public professional sources within your allowed scope, or reports the gaps.
- **Check privacy law.** Handling personal data about people has legal requirements in many places. Make sure your use is allowed.
