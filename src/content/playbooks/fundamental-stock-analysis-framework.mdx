## What This Does

A document-first fundamental analysis process built on one non-negotiable rule: never invent a number. Every figure must trace to a primary source — an annual report, a 10-K, a quarterly filing, a concall transcript — with aggregator sites (screener.in, Yahoo Finance, and similar) treated strictly as navigation aids for *locating* documents, never as a source of record. A recency gate runs before any analysis begins, because the framework's own finding is that the most common way a well-built analysis turns out wrong isn't bad arithmetic — it's a conclusion drawn from data that a newer quarter, rating action, or regulatory decision had already superseded.

The ten-stage workflow classifies the sector first (since standard ratios are undefined or inverted for banks, insurers, REITs, and miners), runs an early kill-criteria and red-flag screen that can escalate straight into a forensic-only mode, reads the full annual report — not just the financial statements, but the risk factors, related-party notes, auditor qualifications, and governance signals that decide most real outcomes — builds a genuinely defensible peer set, and closes with an adversarial challenge pass where a fresh reviewer attacks the draft's own load-bearing claims before it ships. Throughout, the output is explicitly research and analysis, never personalized investment advice or a trading signal.

## Tips & Best Practices

- The recency gate catches the single most common way a technically correct analysis turns out wrong — a conclusion drawn from data already superseded by a newer quarter, rating action, or regulatory decision published days before the analysis date.
- When a red flag surfaces early, resist the pull to keep going with the standard workflow anyway — escalating to a forensic-only pass and saying so explicitly is more useful than a valuation built on earnings nobody should believe.
- The adversarial challenge pass is the step most likely to get skipped under time pressure, and it's exactly the step that catches an analysis that quietly became advocacy for its own conclusion.

## Limitations

- A methodology for equity research and analysis, not licensed financial advice — always state that plainly in any output, and never frame a conclusion as a personalized recommendation.
- Sector-specific metric sets (banks, insurers, REITs, miners, and dozens more) each carry real specialized logic — treat sector specificity as a hard requirement and research the right metric set rather than defaulting to generic ratios.
- Deepest rigor requires primary source documents (annual reports, regulatory filings, transcripts) that may not always be readily accessible — when they aren't, say so explicitly rather than silently substituting aggregator data.
