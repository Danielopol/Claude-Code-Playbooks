## What This Does

A generative method for writing Chinese long-form prose (知乎 answers, 论坛 long posts, 公众号 articles, blogs, 人物 and industry pieces, 科普, tutorials, personal narrative, and fiction) so that it reads as a specific, real person talking rather than a model generating. Its organizing claim is an order of operations most "sound more human" advice skips: the human feel comes first from *material*, second from the *speaking position*, and only last from *tone* — so it refuses to treat the problem as a surface-polish task. The load-bearing move is a material-first gate: before writing anything long, you must hold at least five concrete pieces of material that chain into an actual process, each traceable to something the user said or a verifiable source. If you can't, the method forces a real choice — research it, ask at most three pointed questions, or write something honestly shorter — rather than the default failure mode of padding three thin ideas out to the requested word count with invented examples and the same point explained five ways.

Once material is in hand, it supplies a five-question speaking-position frame (who's talking and what they actually know versus guess; what prompted them; what concrete material backs the piece; what they judge and on what basis; what the reader will ask next) and a set of first-draft craft principles (hit the subject fast, advance every paragraph with something genuinely new, let detail carry emotion instead of explaining it). Only at revision does it apply its anti-AI-slop list — and the key subtlety is that it bans the rhetorical *move*, not the literal phrase: the 不是…而是 reversal setup, four-part parallel restraint, abstract-noun-plus-concrete-verb lyricism, nominalized verbs, em-dashes, signpost colons, and metaphor-wrapped abstractions are all prohibited however they're reworded, because swapping the words while keeping the move leaves the same tell. This makes it a generative companion to a pure after-the-fact style audit, not a duplicate of one.

## Tips & Best Practices

- Expect, and allow, the material gate to push back on length — when the honest answer is "there are only three real things to say here," a tight 600-character piece is the method working correctly, not failing to follow instructions.
- Feed it your actual material up front (your experience, the specific numbers, the real quotes) — the method is explicit that a realistic, lived piece comes from material it can trace, and withholding it just triggers the three-question round.
- Use it as the generative front half and a dedicated AI-tell audit as the back half if you want belt-and-suspenders — this method bans the rhetorical moves while drafting, and a separate line-by-line audit catches anything that slips through.

## Limitations

- Chinese-language-specific by design — the banned constructions (翻案腔, signpost colons, the four-part parallel) and the韵律 it's tuning for are features of Chinese internet long-form prose and don't map onto English AI-writing cleanup.
- It's a writing-craft and editorial discipline, not a fact-checker — for non-fiction it insists every real claim be traceable to user-supplied or verifiable material, but it can't itself verify that a source is accurate; that still needs a real check.
- The method deliberately favors substance over hitting a word count, so it will sometimes deliver less than you asked for when the material doesn't support more — that's a design choice, and a request that genuinely needs length still needs you to supply or allow the research that backs it.
