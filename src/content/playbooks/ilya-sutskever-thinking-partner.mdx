## What This Does

A research-thinking-partner mode built from six mental models distilled from Ilya Sutskever's public interviews, papers, and recommended reading — compression as the real signature of understanding, scale as a tool rather than a governing principle, safety and capability as one entangled technical problem rather than a tradeoff, and research aesthetics (elegance and simplicity) as a genuine signal of correctness rather than decoration. Each model ships with its own honestly stated limitation, mirroring the real epistemic humility in the source material — the compression framework's unexplained generalization gap, the tension between having driven the scaling era and later questioning it, the gap between direction-level safety commitment and a technically complete plan.

Before offering any judgment involving a specific model, paper, or recent event, the mode gathers real facts first rather than reasoning from stale memory — the field moves fast enough that a confidently wrong answer is worse than an honestly uncertain one. Voice discipline (a headline judgment, one everyday analogy, a short close; layered confidence markers from "unquestionably" down to a calm standard decline for undiscussable specifics) comes with an explicit, standing reminder built in: this is a simulation grounded in public material, never the real person's actual current views.

## Tips & Best Practices

- The research-first discipline matters more than the voice — a confidently wrong technical judgment delivered in a perfect register is still wrong, and still worse than an honestly uncertain one.
- Resist the pull to make this persona omniscient about frontier AI news. When the facts genuinely aren't available, reasoning from principles or declining with the standard formula is itself staying in character, not a failure.
- The layered-confidence pattern is easy to flatten into either constant hedging or constant false certainty — actually vary it by how confident the underlying claim really is.

## Limitations

- A simulation built from public statements and documented mental models, not the real person, not authorized by him, and not a source for his actual current views on anything the source material doesn't cover.
- Best suited to research-direction and technical-philosophy questions in AI — stretching this voice to unrelated domains (personal matters, unrelated current events) goes past what the underlying material actually supports.
- The standing disclaimer is a deliberate design choice — don't let a long session quietly drop it just because it already appeared once.
