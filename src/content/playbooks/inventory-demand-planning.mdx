## What This Does

Distills the working knowledge of a senior multi-location retail demand planner into a forecasting-method decision table (moving average, exponential smoothing, Croston's for intermittent demand, causal regression for promotions), a safety-stock methodology that goes beyond the textbook formula to handle lead-time variability and lumpy demand, and a full promotional-planning workflow covering lift estimation, cannibalization, forward-buy, and the post-promo dip that catches unprepared planners off guard.

It also covers ABC/XYZ classification tied to a concrete replenishment policy matrix, seasonal transition timing (buy commitment, markdown triggers, hard liquidation cutoffs), and a set of key edge cases — a viral demand spike, an overnight vendor lead-time doubling, phantom inventory, a holiday calendar shift — each with the specific reasoning that keeps a standard playbook from producing a bad call in that situation.

## Tips & Best Practices

- Document every new-product forecast's assumptions explicitly (which analogs were used, price point, distribution, competitive window) so a post-mortem can audit exactly what was assumed rather than reconstructing it from memory.
- Frame a markdown recommendation to merchandising around sell-through pace and margin math, never as "we bought too much" — the framing changes how the recommendation lands even when the number is identical.
- Watch the tracking signal weekly, not just WMAPE monthly — a demand-pattern regime change (after a reformulation or a competitor's entry) fails a model silently, and the tracking signal is what catches it early.

## Limitations

- Planning methodology, not a forecasting engine — pair it with whatever demand-planning suite, ERP, or spreadsheet actually holds the SKU-level data.
- Dollar figures, lift percentages, and thresholds are illustrative benchmarks from a mid-size multi-location retail context — recalibrate against your own category's actual promo history and margin structure.
- Assumes reasonably clean POS and sell-through data feeding the forecast; garbage-in problems (phantom inventory, miscoded promotions) will silently degrade every downstream number.
