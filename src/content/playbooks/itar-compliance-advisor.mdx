## What This Does

Turns Claude Code into an ITAR compliance advisor for US defense contractors, exporters, and manufacturers. It covers 22 CFR Parts 120-130: United States Munitions List (USML) scoping, DDTC registration, export license applications (DSP-5/73/94), Technical Assistance Agreements (TAA), Manufacturing License Agreements (MLA), brokering (Part 129), deemed-export rules for foreign nationals, technology control plans, voluntary disclosures, violation mitigation, and ITAR-vs-EAR jurisdiction determination.

---

## The Problem

ITAR jurisdiction is unforgiving: classify an item or its technical data wrong and you can commit a criminal violation simply by emailing a drawing or letting a foreign-national employee read it. The license and agreement landscape (DSP-5, TAA, MLA, brokering) is intricate, and deemed-export rules trip up companies that never thought of themselves as "exporters."

## Tips

- **Start with jurisdiction** — ITAR vs. EAR drives everything downstream.
- **Treat technical data like the hardware** — emails, drawings, and source can all be exports.
- **Manage deemed exports** — foreign-national access often needs a license.

## Important Disclaimer

This is a compliance support tool, not legal advice. ITAR violations carry severe civil and criminal penalties. Have qualified export-control counsel review jurisdiction and licensing determinations before acting.
