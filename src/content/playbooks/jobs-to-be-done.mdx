## What This Does

Structured exploration of customer needs across three layers: **jobs** (functional, social, emotional), **pains** (challenges, costliness, mistakes, unresolved problems), and **gains** (expectations, savings, adoption factors, life improvement). Built on Christensen's JTBD theory and Osterwalder's Value Proposition Canvas.

Pairs with `proto-persona`, `problem-statement`, and `positioning-statement`. Feed JTBD outputs into Lean UX hypotheses and epic framing.

## The Three Layers

| Layer | Ask | Example |
|-------|-----|---------|
| **Functional** | "What tasks are you trying to complete?" | Reconcile monthly expenses for tax filing |
| **Social** | "How do you want to be perceived?" | Appear responsive and reliable to clients |
| **Emotional** | "What state do you want to achieve/avoid?" | Feel confident I'm not missing details |

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Verb-driven jobs.** "Send," "analyze," "coordinate" — not "use email."
- **Solution-agnostic.** "Communicate with remote teammates" not "use Slack."
- **Audience-specific social jobs.** Boss, clients, peers — name the audience.
- **Positive + negative emotional jobs.** Both what they seek ("feel in control") and avoid ("avoid embarrassment").
- **Rank pains by intensity.** Ask "if we solved one pain, which would have the biggest impact?"

---

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing jobs with solutions ("I need Slack" is not a job — ask "why?" five times)
- Generic jobs ("be more productive" — dig into which tasks and why)
- Only documenting functional jobs (missing social/emotional motivators)
- Fabricating JTBD from assumptions (use switch interviews)
- Treating all pains as equal (no prioritization → no decision)
