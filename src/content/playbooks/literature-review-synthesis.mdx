## What This Does

This playbook provides a structured workflow for conducting literature reviews. Claude helps you search for relevant papers, extract key information from each, identify themes and gaps, and produce a synthesized review document.

## Prerequisites

- Claude Code installed and configured
- Access to papers (PDFs, URLs, or databases)
- A research topic or question
## Synthesis Matrix

Track themes across papers:

| Paper | Theme A | Theme B | Theme C | Methods | Findings |
|-------|---------|---------|---------|---------|----------|
| Smith 2020 | ✓ | - | ✓ | Survey | X > Y |
| Jones 2021 | ✓ | ✓ | - | Experiment | X = Y |
| Lee 2022 | - | ✓ | ✓ | Interview | X < Y |

## Commands

- `/lit-review [topic]` — Start a new literature review
- "Analyze this paper: [citation/pdf]" — Extract from single paper
- "Synthesize papers on [theme]" — Combine extractions
- "Find gaps in the literature" — Identify what's missing
- "Write the introduction section" — Draft based on synthesis

## Example Usage

**User:**
> "Start a literature review on remote work productivity"

**Claude:**
> ## Literature Review: Remote Work Productivity
>
> ### Phase 1: Scope Definition
>
> **Research Question**: How does remote work affect employee productivity compared to in-office work?
>
> **Inclusion Criteria**:
> - Published 2019-2024 (post-COVID relevant)
> - Empirical studies with productivity measures
> - Knowledge workers / office-type roles
>
> **Exclusion Criteria**:
> - Opinion pieces without data
> - Non-comparable job types (manufacturing, retail)
> - Studies without clear productivity metrics
>
> **Search Terms**:
> - "remote work" AND "productivity"
> - "work from home" AND "performance"
> - "telework" AND "output"
>
> Ready for Phase 2: Search & Collect. Do you have papers to analyze, or should we discuss search strategies?

**Later - Analyzing a paper:**

> **User**: "Analyze this paper: Bloom et al. (2015) 'Does Working from Home Work?'"
>
> **Claude:**
> ## Paper: Bloom 2015 - WFH Experiment
>
> **Citation**: Bloom, N., Liang, J., Roberts, J., & Ying, Z. J. (2015). Does working from home work? Evidence from a Chinese experiment. *Quarterly Journal of Economics*.
>
> **Type**: Randomized experiment
>
> ### Research Question
> Does working from home improve or harm worker productivity?
>
> ### Methodology
> - 9-month RCT at Chinese call center (Ctrip)
> - 249 employees randomized to WFH vs office
> - Productivity = calls handled per minute
>
> ### Key Findings
> 1. WFH increased productivity by 13%
> 2. 9% from working more minutes (fewer breaks, less sick leave)
> 3. 4% from more calls per minute (quieter environment)
> 4. WFH workers reported higher satisfaction
>
> ### Limitations
> - Single company in China
> - Call center work (measurable output)
> - Pre-COVID (voluntary WFH, not forced)
>
> ### Relevance
> Foundational experimental evidence for productivity gains, but limited to specific work type and context.

**Later - Synthesis:**

> **User**: "I've analyzed 8 papers. Synthesize findings on productivity measurement."
>
> **Claude:**
> ## Synthesis: Productivity Measurement Approaches
>
> The literature uses diverse productivity measures:
>
> **Objective Measures** (4 papers):
> - Bloom 2015: Calls per minute
> - Emanuel 2023: Lines of code, commits
> - Gibbs 2023: Tasks completed per hour
>
> **Self-Reported Measures** (3 papers):
> - Barrero 2021: Perceived productivity scale
> - Aksoy 2022: Compared to office baseline
>
> **Manager Assessments** (2 papers):
> - Butler 2021: Performance reviews
>
> **Key Insight**: Papers using objective measures show larger productivity gains (avg +8-13%) than self-reported (+5-8%). This may reflect measurement bias or different constructs.
>
> **Gap Identified**: No papers combine multiple measurement types to validate findings.

## Tips

- **Use parallel analysis**: If you have 5 papers, ask Claude to analyze all 5, then synthesize. Don't do one at a time.
- **Create a synthesis matrix**: Track themes across papers visually. This reveals patterns.
- **Quote sparingly but strategically**: A few key quotes > many quotes.
- **Watch for publication bias**: Studies showing effects are more likely published. Note null findings.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem**: Claude's summaries are too generic

**Solution**: Provide the full paper or detailed excerpts. Abstracts don't have enough detail for good extraction.

**Problem**: Papers seem contradictory

**Solution**: Look for differences in methodology, sample, context. Create a table comparing these factors.

**Problem**: Can't find enough papers

**Solution**: Broaden search terms, extend date range, include related fields. Use citation chaining (papers that cite key papers).
