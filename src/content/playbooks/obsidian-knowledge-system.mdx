## What This Does

Turns a messy Obsidian vault into an organized knowledge system. Claude scans your vault and generates Maps of Content (MOCs), builds a consistent tagging taxonomy, discovers connections between unlinked notes, audits vault health (orphans, duplicates, broken links), and consolidates scattered notes on the same topic into comprehensive pages.

---

## The Problem

Obsidian vaults grow organically and quickly become unmanageable. Notes pile up without structure, tags are inconsistent, related ideas sit in separate files with no links, orphan notes drift into obscurity, and you lose confidence that you can actually find what you know. The vault becomes a graveyard instead of a second brain.

---

## The Fix

Point Claude at your vault and tell it what to fix. It reads your notes, understands the content, and performs structural improvements: generating MOC index pages, normalizing tags, wikilink-connecting related notes, flagging orphans and broken links, and merging fragments into complete notes. Your vault stays in Obsidian — Claude just makes it organized.

## Tips

- **Start with the audit**: Run the vault health check first to see the full picture before making changes
- **MOCs over folders**: Maps of Content are more flexible than folders because a note can appear in multiple MOCs
- **Use hierarchical tags**: Prefer `#project/ml-classifier` over flat tags like `#ml` for better filtering
- **Review before merging**: Always review Claude's consolidation suggestions before it merges notes — context matters
- **Incremental is fine**: You do not need to fix everything at once; tackle one topic cluster per session

## Troubleshooting

**Vault is too large to scan**
Say: "Only scan notes in the [folder name] folder" or "Only scan notes modified in the last 90 days" to limit scope.

**MOCs are too broad**
Ask: "Break the Machine Learning MOC into sub-MOCs: Fundamentals, Techniques, Projects, and Resources."

**Tag normalization changed things I did not want changed**
Undo in Obsidian (Ctrl+Z works on recent changes) or use git if your vault is version-controlled. Next time, say: "Show me the tag changes before applying them."

**Claude suggests linking notes that are not actually related**
This happens with short or ambiguous notes. Say: "Only suggest links where both notes share at least 2 common concepts."
