## What This Does

A thinking-partner mode built from five mental models distilled from Paul Graham's public essays, interviews, and documented history: writing as thinking rather than mere transcription, taste as trainable judgment (with an honest flag about the blind spots any trained taste carries), iterative discovery over up-front design, superlinear returns as a lens for choosing where effort compounds, and independent thinking as a survival skill — including the "keep your identity small" model that explains, in character, why the mode declines to take a side on politics or religion rather than treating that as evasion.

Before offering a judgment on anything involving a specific company, founder, or current market condition, the mode does real research first rather than reasoning from stale memory — matching the actual discipline behind essays that took real investigation, not just opinion. Voice discipline (short sentences, essay-form structure, a real ban on academic padding like "delve" and "utilize," dense analogy use, decisive-on-facts/careful-on-inference certainty) comes with honest, documented limitations built in — including the real pattern of rarely stating a position change as "I was wrong" — and a standing reminder that this is a simulation grounded in public material, never the real person's actual current views.

## Tips & Best Practices

- The research-first discipline is what keeps this from producing confident-sounding but stale takes on anything time-sensitive — don't skip it for questions that actually need current facts.
- When a user pushes on an apparent contradiction, the honest move is to acknowledge the tension directly rather than argue it away — that's part of what makes the voice feel earned rather than defensive.
- Keep the essay-form, open-ended-closing rhythm even under pressure to "wrap up with a summary" — a forced summary paragraph is one of the fastest ways to break the voice.

## Limitations

- A simulation built from public essays, interviews, and documented history — not the real person, not authorized by him, and not a source for his actual current views on anything outside the material it draws from.
- Best suited to startup strategy, writing craft, and general life-decision questions — the further a question drifts from those domains, the less the underlying material actually supports a confident answer.
- The standing disclaimer is a deliberate design choice — don't let a long session quietly drop it just because it already appeared once.
