## What This Does

Ingests quarterly updates and financials across multiple portfolio companies, identifies quick-win AI opportunities at each, and stacks them into a single ranked action list. Designed for quarterly portfolio reviews, annual planning, or deciding which companies get AI investment first.

## What Gets Evaluated

Each company is scored against three gate questions:

1. **Is the data there?** Can they produce a clean input without a 6-month data project first?
2. **Is there an owner?** Someone on the management team who will drive this, not just "support" it.
3. **Can we pilot in 30 days?** One team, one workflow, off-the-shelf tooling.

All three yes means **Go**. Any no means **Wait** with a note on what unblocks it.

## Where AI Typically Hits Hardest

- **Back Office** (fastest to pilot): Invoice processing, contract abstraction, month-end close
- **Revenue / Front Office**: RFP drafts, sales call summaries, support ticket triage, quoting
- **Operations** (sector-dependent): SOP generation, scheduling, code review

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Rank by dollars, not excitement.** A boring AP automation that saves $400k beats a flashy chatbot every time.
- **Off-the-shelf first.** Custom builds are slow and fragile for companies without engineering depth.
- **Hold period drives urgency.** A company 12 months from exit needs something that shows up in LTM EBITDA for the CIM — or skip it entirely.
- **Look for replays.** The highest-leverage move in a portfolio is running one successful play at multiple companies.

## Limitations

- The binding constraint is almost always data quality, not models. If a company cannot produce a clean customer list, AI is not the first project.
- A quick win with no internal owner dies in 90 days. If no one on the management team wants it, mark it Wait regardless of the dollar size.
- Failed pilots are signal. If management already tried something and it didn't stick, find out why before proposing the same thing again.
