## What This Does

Walks through 5 adaptive questions (target → need → category → benefit → differentiation) and outputs a Geoffrey Moore positioning statement plus a one-sentence summary. Each question offers 3-4 enumerated options grounded in your context (B2B/B2C, SMB/enterprise, new/repositioning).

Pairs with `positioning-statement` (output format), `proto-persona`, `jobs-to-be-done`, and `problem-statement`. Feeds into `press-release` and PRD messaging.

## The Flow

| Phase | Question | Output |
|-------|----------|--------|
| **0** | Gather context (website, testimonials, competitive intel) | Grounding material |
| **1** | Who's your target customer? | Specific segment |
| **2** | What underserved need? | JTBD-style pain |
| **3** | What category? | Buyer mental file |
| **4** | What benefit/outcome? | Measurable result |
| **5** | Who's the competitor? | Real alternative + differentiation |
| **Output** | Geoffrey Moore statement | For/that need/is a/that + Unlike/provides |

---

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Use real artifacts.** Paste your website copy, sales objections, competitor pages — don't workshop in a vacuum.
- **Resist consensus.** Positioning forces hard choices; "let's serve both segments" is the failure mode.
- **Outcome-only benefits.** "Has AI" is a feature; "makes decisions 3x faster" is positioning.
- **Name the real competitor.** Excel/spreadsheets/email threads are often the actual alternative — not the competitor you wish you fought.
- **Read aloud to customers.** If they don't say "yes that's me," go back to Q1 or Q2.

---

## Common Pitfalls

- "For everyone" or "for businesses" — invisible to all
- Solution-first answers ("we need better dashboards" instead of root need)
- Category creation without budget for category education
- Feature-list differentiation ("unlike competitors, we have AI")
- Solo PM workshop with no customer validation
