## What This Does

Forces you to turn a product idea into a manual, deliverable-today process before you build anything. Output:

1. A one-sentence description of what the product does manually
2. The "magic piece of paper" — full step-by-step process (trigger → steps → tools → time → handoff)
3. 3 specific people to deliver it to this week
4. Initial price
5. The first step to automate — later, when you're ready

## Quick Start

### Step 1: Download the template

Click **Download** above for the `CLAUDE.md`.

### Step 2: Save into your idea or product folder

Drop it wherever you capture early product thinking.

### Step 3: Run your idea through it

Ask Claude: *"Processize this idea before I build it: [idea]."* It'll produce the manual version and the magic piece of paper.

## The Magic Piece of Paper

Document your manual process so clearly that someone else could run it:

1. **Trigger** — what kicks it off (email, form, message)
2. **Steps** — numbered, exact, in order
3. **Tools needed** — per step
4. **Time per customer** — per step
5. **Handoff** — how you deliver the result

Rule: "I process their request" is not a step. "I open their email, copy the file link, run it through X, format the output, and email it back within 2 hours" is a step.

## Signs You're Ready to Productize

- You've delivered manually to 10+ paying customers
- You can predict exactly what they'll ask for
- The steps haven't changed in weeks
- You're spending more time on delivery than finding customers

Then automate one step at a time — starting with whichever step eats the most time.

## Why This Matters

Skipping the manual phase is how founders spend 6 months building features nobody asked for. The manual version teaches you which steps are actually bottlenecks, which edge cases matter, and which parts customers don't even notice.

## Who Should Use This

Idea-stage founders itching to open a code editor, service providers transitioning to products, anyone who's been told "talk to customers" and doesn't know how to structure the first ten conversations.
