## What This Does

Walks through 10 sections (business outcome → product outcome → problem → hypothesis → positioning → assumptions → PESTEL → value justification → SMART metrics → next steps). Built specifically for AI features where uncertainty is high and execs want explicit risk framing.

Pairs with `problem-statement`, `epic-hypothesis`, `positioning-statement`, `proto-persona`, and `pestel-analysis`.

## The 10 Sections

| # | Section | Output |
|---|---------|--------|
| **1** | Business Outcome | [Direction] [Metric] [Outcome] [Context] |
| **2** | Product Outcome | Customer POV outcome |
| **3** | Problem Statement | Persona narrative |
| **4** | Solution Hypothesis | If/Then + experiments + Proof-of-Life |
| **5** | Positioning | Value prop + differentiation |
| **6** | Assumptions & Unknowns | Explicit list to validate |
| **7** | PESTEL Risks | Investigate + Monitor |
| **8** | Value Justification | C-level convincing |
| **9** | Success Metrics | SMART |
| **10** | What's Next | 5 strategic steps |

---

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Business + customer outcomes both required.** One without the other is incomplete.
- **Hypothesis = bet, not commitment.** Frame as "if we... then we will..." with experiments to validate.
- **PESTEL specificity matters.** "Regulations might change" is theater. "GDPR for storing email timing patterns requires legal review" is actionable.
- **Tiny Acts of Discovery before engineering.** 2-week prototype + 10 users beats 3-month build for a hypothesis.
- **Proof-of-Life criteria upfront.** Define quant + qual signals before testing, not after.

---

## Common Pitfalls

- Vague outcomes ("improve UX," "increase revenue")
- Solution-first problem framing ("we need AI X")
- Skipping Tiny Acts of Discovery (hypothesis straight to roadmap)
- Generic PESTEL risks ("regulations might change")
- Value justification without data or dollar amounts
