## What This Does

Designs the systems that connect marketing, sales, and customer success — covering lead lifecycle, scoring, routing, MQL/SQL handoff, deal desk, CRM automation, and data hygiene — so leads don't leak, reports don't lie, and revenue forecasts are actually trustworthy.

4. Design routing rules (territory, segment, round-robin, account-based)

## Give It Real Data, Not Descriptions

The template's scoring advice hinges on one step: test the model against deals you've already won and lost. Claude can't do that from a description of your funnel, so export the data before you start:

1. **Closed-won and closed-lost opportunities from the last 12–18 months** as CSV: company size, industry, lead source, job title of the first contact, days in each stage, and the outcome.
2. **Your current lead records** with lifecycle stage, owner and created date. This exposes unassigned and stale leads.
3. **Your current MQL definition and SLA**, even if it's informal ("marketing sends over anyone who books a demo").

Put the files in the project folder and ask: *"Compare the attributes of won vs. lost deals, propose fit and engagement weights, and show me which past deals the model would have scored as MQLs."* That backtest is the difference between a scoring model sales trusts and one they ignore.

## What a First Scoring Model Looks Like

For a sales-led B2B product with a mid-five-figure ACV, a first pass often looks like this (your weights should come from your own backtest):

| Signal | Type | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Company size in ICP range | Fit | +20 |
| Director-level or above in buying department | Fit | +15 |
| Visited pricing page twice in 14 days | Engagement | +20 |
| Requested a demo | Engagement | +30 |
| Downloaded an ebook | Engagement | +5 |
| Personal email domain | Negative | −20 |
| Competitor domain | Negative | −100 |

With an MQL threshold around 60, a demo request from an in-ICP director qualifies on its own, while someone collecting ebooks never does. That's the template's "fit **and** engagement" rule made concrete.

## Rollout Advice

- **Get the definitions signed off before anyone builds a workflow.** The template's "define before automate" principle is the one most teams skip. Ask Claude for a one-page lifecycle document to circulate first.
- **Start rejection reason codes on day one.** The feedback loop from rejected MQLs is what lets you recalibrate the model each quarter.
- **Watch speed-to-lead first.** It's the metric that responds fastest to routing changes, so it shows within weeks whether the new rules work.
