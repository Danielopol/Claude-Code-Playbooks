## What This Does

A sentence-level academic English editor that reviews across five independently-rated dimensions — grammar, word choice, voice, coherence/cohesion, and sentence structure — each backed by a concrete before/after table rather than general advice to "sound more academic." The grammar section catches specific, common errors (subject-verb disagreement on collective nouns, dangling modifiers, broken parallelism, "that" vs. "which") with a correction and a one-line reason for each; the word-choice section maps dozens of colloquial phrases ("a lot of," "get," "deal with") to their academic equivalents while explicitly warning against over-substitution ("can" doesn't need replacing); and a full tense-by-section table specifies exactly when a paper should shift from past tense (describing what was done) to present tense (stating a conclusion) across the Abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, and Conclusion.

The voice and coherence sections are where it goes beyond a basic grammar check: a scenario table specifies exactly when active voice belongs ("We trained the model," describing the authors' own actions) versus passive ("The samples were analyzed," emphasizing the object of an action), and a signal-words table maps each logical relationship — addition, contrast, cause-and-effect, concession, exemplification — to its correct transition word, catching the common mistake of using "Furthermore" where the sentence actually expresses a contrast that needs "However." Sentence-structure guidance covers concrete fixes for run-on academic sentences (past ~40 words, split), noun pile-ups, and starting sentences with "There is/are," rounding out with section-specific structural conventions for the Abstract (a five-part funnel in ~150–300 words) and Introduction (broad context → specific problem → limitations of existing work → contribution → outline).

## Tips & Best Practices

- Specify the target journal or field when you have one — regional punctuation conventions (American vs. British quotation-mark placement) and formality expectations vary enough between venues that this changes real decisions.
- Don't over-apply the colloquial-to-academic substitutions — the guide itself flags "can" as acceptable as-is; blanket-replacing every simple verb with a longer Latinate one produces stiff, over-formal prose rather than better academic writing.
- Use the coherence section specifically when a reviewer or advisor says a section "doesn't flow" — the jumping-argumentation and signal-word-misuse diagnoses name the actual structural problem instead of leaving you to guess what "doesn't flow" means.

## Limitations

- A sentence- and paragraph-level polish, not a structural or claim-evidence review — pairing it with a structural editing pass (checking whether the paper's overall argument and evidence hold together) covers a different, complementary layer.
- Section-specific conventions (abstract length, tense patterns) are general academic norms; always defer to a specific target journal's actual author guidelines where they differ.
- Voice and tense guidance reflects general STEM/social-science academic convention; some fields (humanities, certain qualitative research traditions) have different, sometimes contrary, stylistic norms.
