## What This Does

Most ideas have been tried. Some are solved well, some are solved badly, some failed for instructive reasons, and a few are genuinely open. This skill figures out which bucket your idea falls into and tells you plainly — so you don't burn a weekend (or a quarter) reinventing something that already exists.

It triggers automatically whenever you describe something you're thinking of building ("I want to build…", "I'm thinking of making…", "should I build X…"), runs a structured prior-art search across GitHub, Hacker News, Product Hunt, package registries, and domain-specific venues, and delivers one of five verdicts: **Build it**, **Fork/extend**, **Contribute upstream**, **Use existing**, or **Investigate first**.

## How It Works

The skill follows an 8-step core loop:

1. **Restate** the idea in one sentence and confirm with you.
2. **Generate vocabulary** — 6–10 framings from distinct vantage points (builder, user, academic, infrastructure, adjacent community) before searching, so queries don't all hit the same semantic cluster.
3. **Search where builders publish** — GitHub, Hacker News (Show HN), Product Hunt, PyPI/npm/crates.io, Hugging Face, arXiv, plus domain-specific venues.
4. **Trace one layer down** on every direct match — the project you found may be a thin wrapper over the real incumbent.
5. **Cluster** findings into Direct / Adjacent / Partial / Abandoned.
6. **Extract standard patterns** — what shows up repeatedly across matches.
7. **Benchmark your angle** honestly. Name the real differentiator, or admit there isn't one.
8. **Deliver a verdict** — one of five, with a concrete next step.

Budget: ≤10 total search queries.

## Output Format

Every run produces a short markdown report with these sections:

- **The idea, restated** (one sentence)
- **The landscape** (table grouped by bucket — max 3 rows per bucket)
- **Standard patterns** (3–6 bullets)
- **Differentiator analysis** (one honest paragraph)
- **Verdict** — Build it / Fork X / Contribute to Y / Use Z / Investigate first

The whole report fits on one screen unless the landscape is unusually rich.

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Be specific about the constraint that makes your idea distinctive.** "Migration tool" is too vague; "migration tool that reads OpenAPI specs and emits reversible Postgres migrations" gives the skill something to benchmark against.
- **Don't fight the verdict if it says "use existing".** That's the skill working — it just saved you a quarter.
- **Treat "Investigate first" as a green light to read closed issues and post-mortems**, not a red light. The most useful prior art is often a project that died.
- **Re-run after pivots.** A small framing change ("for SQL" vs "for Prisma") can completely change the landscape.

## What This Skill Avoids

- **Inventing competitors.** If the search comes up empty after real effort, it says "no direct prior art found" and tells you where it looked.
- **Padded verdicts.** No "it depends, here are considerations…" — the skill picks one verdict.
- **Stopping at the first match.** Finding one tool doesn't mean the idea is dead; it means you now know the baseline.
- **Diplomatic hedging.** You asked for a reality check. It gives one.

## Example Verdicts (Tone Reference)

**Already built, use it:**
> **Verdict: Use existing — Alembic.** Your idea is exactly what Alembic does, and it's been the standard for a decade. Your YAML twist is real but small; it's a fork, not a new project.

**Genuine gap:**
> **Verdict: Build it.** Three adjacent tools exist but none do API-schema-first migrations with rollback safety checks. The closest (prisma-migrate) is tied to Prisma. Real gap.

**Someone tried, it died:**
> **Verdict: Investigate first.** schema-forge (2021, archived) did almost exactly this with 1.2k stars but no commits in 3 years. Read the closed issues before rebuilding.
